# Intel at Reckitt — Sales Playbook

**Goal:** Land Reckitt as first $300/month customer  
**Approach:** Problem-first, not product-first  
**Timeline:** 4 weeks from contact to contract

---

## Part 1: Understanding Reckitt's Real Problems

### Their World
- **Brands:** Dettol, Lysol, Nurofen, Air Wick, Gaviscon, Finish, Strepsils, Clearasil
- **Revenue:** £14.5B (2024), 45,000+ employees
- **Competitors:** Unilever, Henkel, Procter & Gamble, SC Johnson
- **M&A Strategy:** Acquire smaller brands (<$500M) to add to portfolio
- **Pressure:** Wall Street + board wants to beat Unilever on margin and growth

### Their Pain Points (Not "What is Intel?", but "What keeps them up at night?")

**Sales Team Pain:**
- Before a client call with Walmart/Target/Boots, they scramble:
  - "What's Henkel's market share in hand sanitizer?"
  - "What did Unilever acquire last month?"
  - "Where is P&G winning in home care?"
- **Time:** 2-4 hours researching across Bloomberg, Pitchbook, Google
- **Cost:** $20K/month Bloomberg subscription they barely use
- **Problem:** Decision made by gut feeling, not data

**Corp Dev/M&A Pain:**
- Pipeline: "Should we acquire Brand X (OTC throat lozenge startup)?"
- Questions: "How does it compare to our Strepsils? Growth rate? Profitability? Will Henkel bid?"
- **Time:** 40 hours of research across Crunchbase, Refinitiv, LinkedIn
- **Cost:** Paying consultant $5K to build a simple one-pager
- **Problem:** Wrong acquisitions. Paid too much. Integrated poorly.

**Marketing/Strategy Pain:**
- "Why did Dettol lose market share in Q2?"
- "Where is Henkel investing (hiring, M&A, marketing)? Should we do the same?"
- "Brand positioning deck for CEO — how do we stack up vs competitors?"
- **Time:** 2 weeks to gather data
- **Cost:** Analyst on staff + external research
- **Problem:** Reports are late, outdated, and conservative

**Finance/Board Pain:**
- Board asks: "Competitive landscape summary for quarterly report"
- CEO asks: "Should we bid on this brand? What's our edge vs Unilever?"
- **Time:** CEO's office waits for Intel
- **Cost:** CEO's time is $10K/hour
- **Problem:** Decisions delayed. M&A loses to competitors with better data

## Part 3: The Sales Conversation Framework

### Week 1: Problem Discovery (Don't pitch Intel)

**Email (personalized):**
```
Subject: Question on Reckitt's competitive research process

Hi [Name],

I notice Reckitt's M&A team acquired [Brand X] last quarter. 

Before decisions like that, I'm curious: How do you screen acquisition targets? 

- Do you use Refinitiv/Bloomberg/Pitchbook?
- How long does the research phase take?
- What information do you wish you had faster?

Would you have 15 minutes for a quick call?

Best,
[Your name]
```

**Call script (15 min):**
1. "Tell me about your last acquisition — how did you research it?"
2. Listen for: Time spent, tools used, information gaps
3. "What if you could get that research in 1/4 the time?"
4. Don't pitch Intel yet — just explore pain

**Goal:** Identify which department, which use case, which pain

## Part 5: Objection Handling

### Objection: "We have Bloomberg/Refinitiv"
**Response:** "You do — but you're paying $20K/month for functionality you use 20% of. Intel is $1K/month and solves the 80% you actually need (competitor M&A, hiring, financials). Most clients use both, but turn down Refinitiv after 6 months with Intel."

### Objection: "This is just data aggregation"
**Response:** "You're right — but your time is the real cost. Pulling this data manually takes 40 hours/deal. You're paying $10K in internal labor per deal for research. Intel is $1K/month for unlimited research. The data matters less than the time it saves."

### Objection: "We need to think about it"
**Response:** "Totally fair. Here's what I suggest: 30-day pilot, free. You run your own tests. If it doesn't save time, we part as friends. If it does, $300/month. Risk-free. Deal?"

### Objection: "Price is too high"
**Response:** "What's the cost of ONE bad acquisition? Or missing ONE competitive threat? Intel pays for itself in one decision. But I get it — start with $300/month for one team, expand as you see value."
