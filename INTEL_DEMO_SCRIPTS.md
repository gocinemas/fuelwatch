## Demo 1: Sales Team Perspective ⭐ PROVEN

**Objective:** Show how a salesperson prepares better for client calls by understanding competitor strategy.

**Setup:** You're a sales rep at Unilever, preparing for a strategic call with a P&G buyer.

### Demo Flow

**Opening (30 seconds)**
> "Before this call, I need to understand what P&G is doing differently. Let me show you how Intel changes that."

**Step 1: Search Your Prospect (1 minute)**
1. Go to search page: search for "Procter & Gamble"
2. Point out:
   - **Revenue:** $80.2B (large, stable company)
   - **Hiring Rate:** +8.5% (actively expanding teams)
   - **Stock Momentum:** 58% (warm, not hot — defensive positioning)
3. **Say:** "P&G is profitable but playing it safe. They're hiring but not aggressively."

**Step 2: Compare with Self (2 minutes)**
1. Click "Compare" button
2. Add: Unilever, Reckitt, Henkel
3. Show metrics table:
   - **Revenue Growth:** P&G 2.1% vs Unilever 3.8% vs Reckitt 5.2% (Reckitt is most aggressive)
   - **Operating Margin:** P&G 22.5% vs Unilever 18.2% (P&G is a profit machine)
   - **Revenue per Employee:** P&G $4.2M vs Unilever $3.8M (P&G operates lean)
4. **Say:** "P&G is more profitable and more efficient with headcount. They're optimizing, not expanding."

**Step 3: Check M&A Activity (1 minute)**
1. Scroll to M&A timeline
2. Point to last 5 P&G deals:
   - "Billie razors" (DTC, $915M) — went for younger women
   - "Merck Consumer Health" ($4.1B) — acquired OTC portfolio
   - "Coty Fragrances" ($12B) — divested fragrances, kept OTC
3. **Say:** "P&G is consolidating. They divested fragrances but doubled down on OTC health. This tells me they're betting on pharma, not beauty."

**Step 4: Conversation Insight (30 seconds)**
> "So when I call P&G today, I know:
> - They're optimizing for margin, not growth
> - They're consolidating into healthcare
> - They won't be aggressive on beauty acquisitions
> 
> My pitch should focus on margin uplift and OTC opportunities, not on growth stories."

**Closing Question for Audience:**
> "Without this, what would you guess about P&G's strategy? Now you know in 3 minutes."

### Success Metrics
- ✅ Identified 3 key strategic differences
- ✅ Found 2 recent M&A deals that indicate direction
- ✅ Actionable insight for the call
- ✅ Time: 3 minutes (beats internal analyst request)

## Demo 2: Corporate Development Strategy 🎯 POWER MOVE

**Objective:** Show how to evaluate M&A targets and understand if a company is "in play" based on acquisition appetite and strategic direction.

**Setup:** You're in Corp Dev at Henkel, screening acquisition targets in the beauty/health space.

### Demo Flow

**Opening (30 seconds)**
> "We're looking to acquire 3-5 brands in premium beauty over the next 18 months. Usual process: 40 candidates, 3-month analysis. Intel cuts this to 8 candidates in 3 minutes."

**Step 1: Load the Competitor Set (1 minute)**
1. Go to comparison page
2. Pre-load: Henkel, Unilever, Reckitt, Nestlé, S.C. Johnson
3. Show metrics table:
   - **Revenue Growth:** Reckitt 5.2% > Unilever 3.8% > Henkel 2.1%
   - **Stock Momentum:** Reckitt 72% (hot) vs Henkel 48% (cold)
   - **Hiring:** Reckitt +12% vs Henkel +3.5%
4. **Say:** "Reckitt is the most aggressive buyer. Highest valuation risk but fastest growing."

**Step 2: Deep Dive: Reckitt M&A (2 minutes)**
1. Click on Reckitt in comparison
2. Scroll M&A timeline — last 5 deals:
   - **Recent:** GC Pharma acquisition ($320M, Feb 2025) — OTC pharma
   - **2024:** Monistat ($385M) — women's health
   - **2024:** AI Health Analytics investment ($50M) — digital health
   - **2023:** Clearasil ($280M) — skincare
   - **2020:** Lysol expansion ($600M) — disinfectants
3. **Pattern:** Strategic focus on OTC health + women's health + AI
4. **Say:** "Reckitt's M&A playbook: 
   - They acquire brands, not companies
   - Average deal size: $300-400M
   - Focus: women's health + OTC + digital
   - If you want to sell a brand to them, it fits that profile"

**Step 3: Competitive Threat Assessment (1 minute)**
1. Compare Henkel vs Reckitt hiring and M&A:
   - Henkel: 3 deals in last 2 years ($1.3B total)
   - Reckitt: 6 deals in last 2 years ($2.8B total)
2. **Say:** "Reckitt has 2.2x the acquisition velocity. They're competitive, they're funded, they're serious."

**Step 4: Decision Framework (30 seconds)**
> "For each target brand, ask:
> - Does Reckitt already own it? (Check deals) → If yes, high competition
> - Is it in their M&A pattern? (Health, women's health, digital) → If yes, they'll bid higher
> - Can we move faster? (Compare our hiring) → If no, they'll outbid us
> 
> Use this to prioritize: go after brands Reckitt isn't targeting yet."

**Closing Question for Audience:**
> "Without M&A history, you'd assume all competitors bid the same way. Now you know each has a playbook — and Reckitt is the aggressive one."

### Success Metrics
- ✅ Ranked competitors by acquisition appetite
- ✅ Identified strategic focus areas
- ✅ Competitive threat assessment
- ✅ Decision framework for target prioritization
- ✅ Time: 4 minutes (beats internal analyst + external consultant)

## Demo 3: Private Equity Thesis 🏛️ BOARD-LEVEL

**Objective:** Show how to assess portfolio company competitive position and identify exit risks or upsell opportunities.

**Setup:** You're a PE partner managing a $500M revenue portfolio company in the CPG space (S.C. Johnson). You need to brief the board on competitive positioning and exit readiness.

### Demo Flow

**Opening (1 minute)**
> "We own S.C. Johnson. Market cap opportunity: $8-12B. Our question: Is it ready for sale? What's the competitive landscape?
> 
> Three years ago, this would require:
> - Hiring a consultant firm ($150K+)
> - 4-week project
> - 80-page report
> 
> Here's the answer in 5 minutes."

**Step 1: Portfolio Company Profile (1 minute)**
1. Load S.C. Johnson
2. Show key stats:
   - **Revenue:** $11.2B (mid-market, substantial)
   - **Operating Margin:** 18.5% (healthy, but not world-class)
   - **Employees:** 13,000 (lean operation)
   - **Stock Momentum:** 55% (lukewarm; not exciting to buyers)
   - **Hiring:** +5% (steady, not aggressive)
3. **Say:** "S.C. Johnson is stable and profitable, but sleeping. It's not growing fast enough to excite growth investors."

**Step 2: Competitive Positioning (2 minutes)**
1. Compare: S.C. Johnson vs Henkel vs Reckitt vs Unilever vs P&G
2. Show metrics:
   - **Profitability:** S.C. Johnson 18.5% vs P&G 22.5% (gap: 4%)
   - **Growth:** S.C. Johnson 1.8% vs Reckitt 5.2% (gap: 3.4%)
   - **Revenue/Emp:** S.C. Johnson $0.86M vs P&G $4.2M (gap: massive — SCJ is inefficient)
3. **Charts Show:**
   - Growth Trajectory: SCJ flat, Reckitt climbing
   - Operating Margin: P&G #1, Henkel #2, SCJ #4 (margin compression risk)
4. **Say:** "S.C. Johnson is the smallest and has the worst efficiency metrics. For a buyer:
   - If they want growth: SCJ is too slow (see Reckitt's momentum)
   - If they want margin: P&G is better (see P&G at 22.5%)
   - If they want restructuring upside: Yes, SCJ has it (cut 20% headcount, improve efficiency)
   
   But restructuring plays are risky. Easier to buy Henkel (already growing) than to fix SCJ."

**Step 3: M&A Market Reality (1 minute)**
1. Scroll M&A timeline (all 5 companies):
   - **SCJ:** 2 deals in 2 years ($180M total) — essentially dormant
   - **Henkel:** 5 deals in 2 years ($1.5B total)
   - **Reckitt:** 6 deals in 2 years ($2.8B total)
   - **Unilever:** 4 deals in 2 years ($3.2B total)
   - **P&G:** 3 deals in 2 years ($1.2B total)
2. **Say:** "SCJ isn't in the acquisition game. It's not buying or being bought. That's a red flag for exit.
   
   Compare to Reckitt: 6 deals in 24 months. That's a company in growth mode.
   Compare to Unilever: $3.2B deployed in 2 years. That's a buyer with capital.
   
   If you sell SCJ, who buys it? Unilever or Reckitt? But they're already building — they won't overpay for a sleepy company."

**Step 4: Board Recommendation (30 seconds)**
> **Exit Strategy:**
> - **Good news:** SCJ is profitable, stable, low debt. Clean business.
> - **Bad news:** Not growth-oriented. Buyers prefer Henkel or Reckitt (growing).
> - **Path to higher exit:** Increase operating margin (labor efficiency) or revenue growth (new geographies or M&A into SCJ)
> - **Timeline:** 2-3 years to improve attractiveness, or accept lower multiple now.

**Closing Question for Audience:**
> "What's the competitive position of your portfolio company?
> - Growing fast like Reckitt? Sell in 18 months to a strategic buyer for premium.
> - Stable like SCJ? Invest 2-3 years in margin improvement, then sell.
> - Declining? Get out now before it's worth less.
> 
> Intel tells you which bucket you're in and what levers you have."

### Success Metrics
- ✅ Portfolio company health assessment
- ✅ Competitive positioning analysis
- ✅ M&A market appetite evaluation
- ✅ Exit timing recommendation
- ✅ Strategic options identified
- ✅ Time: 5 minutes (board-ready analysis)

**Q: How often does it update?**

**Q: Can I search any company?**
> Phase 1: 14 companies (large, established players in CPG, pharma, tech). Phase 2: 100+ companies with category selection.
